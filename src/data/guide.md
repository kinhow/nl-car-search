# Used-car buyer's guide (demo knowledge base)

Illustrative notes for this demo. Rules change, so check JPJ, APAD, Puspakom and your insurer before you buy.

## road-tax-petrol: Road tax for petrol and diesel cars (Peninsular Malaysia)
Road tax for a privately owned car in Peninsular Malaysia is based on engine capacity (cc). Up to 1,000cc: RM20 a year. 1,001–1,200cc: RM55. 1,201–1,400cc: RM70. 1,401–1,600cc: RM90. 1,601–1,800cc: RM200 plus RM0.40 for every cc above 1,600. 1,801–2,000cc: RM280 plus RM0.50 for every cc above 1,800. 2,001–2,500cc: RM380 plus RM1.00 for every cc above 2,000. 2,501–3,000cc: RM880 plus RM2.50 for every cc above 2,500. So a 1.5-litre car costs RM90 a year and a 2.0-litre car costs RM380.

## road-tax-east: Road tax in Sabah and Sarawak
Road tax in Sabah and Sarawak is lower than in Peninsular Malaysia for the same engine size. Check the JPJ calculator for the exact amount.

## road-tax-ev: Road tax for electric cars
Electric cars are taxed on motor power in kilowatts (kW), not engine cc. The full exemption for EVs has ended, so check the current JPJ rate for the exact motor rating before you buy.

## transfer: Transferring ownership
To transfer a used car, the car must pass a Puspakom B5 ownership-transfer inspection, which checks that the chassis and engine numbers match the registration. Buyer and seller then complete the transfer through JPJ or MyJPJ with biometric verification. If the car still has a loan, the seller's bank must settle it before ownership can move.

## loan: Car loans (hire purchase)
Used-car loans in Malaysia are usually hire purchase with a fixed flat interest rate. Banks typically finance up to 90% of the price and allow up to 9 years, though older cars often get shorter tenures and smaller margins. Total interest = price financed × flat rate × years.

## insurance-ncd: Insurance and No Claim Discount (NCD)
Car insurance is required to renew road tax. The No Claim Discount grows with each claim-free year: 25% after 1 year, 30% after 2, 38.33% after 3, 45% after 4 and 55% after 5 or more. NCD belongs to the driver, not the car, and moves with you to your next car.

## mileage: What counts as normal mileage
Malaysian drivers typically cover about 20,000 to 25,000 km a year. A car well under that for its age is low mileage. A very low reading on an old car is worth checking against service records, because odometers can be tampered with.

## ehailing: Using a car for e-hailing
E-hailing drivers need an EVP (e-hailing vehicle permit) and a PSV licence, and the car must have e-hailing insurance cover. The car must be under the maximum age set by APAD (check the current limit) and pass a Puspakom inspection. Sedans and MPVs with good rear legroom and low running costs are the usual choice.

## flood: Spotting a flood-damaged car
Signs of flood damage: a musty smell, rust under the seats and on seat rails, water lines or mud inside the boot and spare-tyre well, fogged headlamps, and electrical faults. Ask for an independent inspection, especially for cars from flood-prone areas after the monsoon season.

## service: Servicing and running costs
Most petrol cars need servicing every 10,000 km or 6 months, whichever comes first. National brands (Perodua, Proton) have the cheapest parts and the most workshops. Continental brands cost noticeably more to maintain once the warranty ends. EVs have far fewer service items, but tyre wear and battery health matter.

## inspection: Inspecting before you buy
Check the service record, look for uneven panel gaps and repainted panels (signs of an accident), test-drive from a cold start, and listen for gearbox jerks. A paid third-party inspection costs a few hundred ringgit and is worth it on any car over RM50k.
